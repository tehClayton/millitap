Timing is measured against the audio clock, and the browser's reported output latency is already subtracted. Use this only if your gear adds delay the browser can't see — Bluetooth headphones add 100–300 ms and report it unreliably. Wired or the built-in speaker gives far cleaner numbers.

If your average sits 20–50 ms ahead, that's probably real. Tapping slightly early against a metronome is a well-documented perceptual effect, not a fault in your playing or your offset. Don't dial it out — it's one of the things worth watching.

This answers whether Safari's touch timestamps come from the hardware or from whenever the page got round to handling them. Run a normal drill and note your spread. Turn this on and run the same drill: it will deliberately stall the page, and the lag figure will climb into the tens of milliseconds. If lag climbs but your spread doesn't, the timestamps are hardware-derived and page stalls can't corrupt your readings. If spread climbs with it, the web version has a ceiling and native is the fix.
